# Evidence-led bullets and openings

Start with the role's central requirements and the match map. Select the strongest supported decisions, actions, analyses, artifacts, responsibilities, and observed outcomes. Preserve personal versus team contribution, direct versus transferable fit, and disclosure boundaries. Qualitative outcomes are useful. Never invent metrics or present intended benefits as achieved results.

Order evidence within each employer by relevance and proof strength, while preserving approved chronology and structure. Each bullet should add distinct evidence. Prefer precise verbs, but do not enforce a candidate-specific verb, heading, punctuation, or page-count rule. Relevant older experience may supply stronger proof than a recent generic claim.

A resume opening should connect the employer's mandate, the candidate's supported contribution, and a credible customer, team, or business payoff. Match the candidate's approved voice. Before presentation, briefly critique generic language, posting imitation, unsupported scope, and a missing payoff. No separate reviewer or decision-card file is required by default.

A cover opening should express a plausible, specific reason for wanting the work and connect it to supported contribution. Do not explain the company back to itself or invent passion, familiarity, or expertise. Ask one focused interest question only when existing preferences and evidence do not support a convincing reason.
