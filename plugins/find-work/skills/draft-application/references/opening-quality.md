# Resume opening quality

Read this before drafting or substantively revising a resume opening or summary. It runs in the current context with no separate reviewer or decision-card file. The candidate's `profile/resume_preferences.md` overrides any default here.

## Build it

State privately the role's primary mandate, what the candidate makes better for the team or its decisions, and the resulting customer or business payoff. Find the approved opening whose argument best fits that combination. Reuse it when its fit and claims hold, adapt it as little as necessary when close, or write a new one when approved openings miss something important.

## Tests before presenting

- **Concrete payoff.** It says what becomes better because this candidate joins, concretely enough to picture. A philosophy alone fails.
- **One thread.** It follows one idea. Strong ideas still fail when there are too many of them and the opening jumps between them. Simplify the leaps between ideas, not the vocabulary, and never compress until the reason behind a claim disappears.
- **Not generic.** Reject it if almost any candidate for the role could say it unchanged.
- **Not the posting.** Reject it if it mirrors recognizable posting language. A small amount of distinctive, broadly applicable wording from the posting may appear naturally. It should feel familiar, not conspicuously tailored, and must not name the employer's products or proprietary concepts.
- **Banned openings.** Never use an opening, or a close paraphrase, listed under banned openings in the candidate's resume preferences or approved openings library.
- **Cold read.** Read it as a stranger who knows only the job posting. If a reader would need the drafting context to follow it, simplify before presenting.

Keep supporting proof in the bullets, not in the opening. Do not repeat the skills section.
