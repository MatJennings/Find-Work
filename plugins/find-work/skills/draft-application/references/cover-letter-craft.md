# Cover-letter craft

Read this before drafting or substantively revising a cover letter. It is the default letter procedure and runs in the current context with no extra agents or saved records. The candidate's `profile/cover_letter_preferences.md` overrides any default here.

## Purpose

The resume establishes credibility. The letter establishes relevance, judgment and genuine interest, so it should not retell the resume. A competent, polished letter that matches the posting is necessary but not enough. Give the reader a specific reason to want a conversation with the candidate.

## Argument before proof

Choose the argument before choosing evidence. State, in a brief private drafting summary:

1. The employer's mandate, the problem this role exists to solve, with an exact posting quote.
2. The candidate's supported point of view or way of working that is most relevant to it. Prefer what reveals how the candidate thinks over what merely repeats the closest resume bullet.
3. The practical difference that point of view would make for this employer.
4. The one supported proof point that best earns it.

Starting from the most similar past letter or resume bullet is not a substitute for this step. Do not save private alternatives.

## Thesis first, in one pass

Before writing the letter, draft three genuinely different opening theses. Each is the actual first sentence in the candidate's voice, with its central idea, the employer anchor it builds on, and its one proof point. When the approved openings library holds a fitting idea, one thesis may adapt it to this role. Score each thesis 1 to 5 on these criteria.

- Boldness. Is it a definitive claim with a point of view?
- Pull. Would a hiring manager want to know why the candidate believes it?
- Clarity. Does a cold reader understand it immediately?
- Employer fit. Does it build on what this employer most cares about, at this role's level?
- Provability. Can the letter earn it from supported evidence? An opinion may sit near the evidence but not far beyond it. Statements of what the candidate has done always need support.

Pick the winner, discard any thesis the evidence cannot reach, and write the letter around it. Every later sentence should help prove the opening thesis. Present only the winner unless two are genuinely competitive. This is a self-review in one context, not an independent review, and must not be described as one.

## Shape

Unless the candidate's preferences say otherwise, use three short body paragraphs, occasionally four, of about 175 to 300 words.

1. The problem and why the candidate wants it. An observation or personal point of view may open the letter, but connect it to the role within the first paragraph. Frame an inference about the employer as an outside reading when it could be mistaken for inside knowledge.
2. How the candidate would approach it. One to three sentences on first moves, questions or judgment. Prospective wording such as "I'd" makes the hypothetical status clear. Never prescribe the employer's strategy as if its internal situation were known.
3. One proof point, told through the decision or judgment behind it rather than a list of activities. Then a brief close.

Spend more words on how the candidate would approach the work than on what they have done. Do not list several employers or summarize the career.

## Opening

Answer "Why is this the work I want to do?", not "What does this company do?" Use company or posting details as evidence of interest, never as a company overview. If the paragraph could be mistaken for an outside description of the employer with the candidate's name removed, rewrite it. Avoid "I am writing to apply" and flattery without substance.

## Read-aloud check

Before presenting, silently read the whole body as if the candidate were saying it to a person. Check that each thought leads to the next, pronouns are clear and every sentence can be spoken comfortably. Prefer ordinary verbs, contractions where they fit the candidate's voice, and concrete situations over stacked abstract nouns. Short sentences alone do not make speech. Humor or cleverness should come from a real observation and serve the argument. Never invent dialogue, incidents or outcomes to make the letter entertaining. This is an editorial simulation, never a claim of actual audio testing.

## Closing

Reaffirm interest briefly and state the value the candidate could bring. If the candidate's preferences ask for it and the employer has other openings in a similar area, add one sentence welcoming consideration for related positions, without implying other applications or fit for every opening.

## Deterministic check

When Python is available, run `<plugin>/scripts/check_letter_clarity.py <wording file>` with the length and punctuation flags that match the candidate's preferences. It flags stock phrases, length and paragraph count, selected punctuation, and eight-word runs repeated from the resume in the same file. It backs up, never replaces, the semantic checks above. When Python is unavailable, check these items by reading.
